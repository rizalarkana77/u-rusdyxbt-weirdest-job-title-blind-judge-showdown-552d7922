## The title

> Senior Vice President of Waiting Room Magazines

The most ordinary corporate title there is — the kind on a hundred LinkedIn
profiles a day — in charge of the most neglected print on earth: the stack of
magazines in a waiting room, last renewed sometime in 2013. Nobody reads
them, nobody throws them away, and now somebody senior is in charge of them.

## Why this title wins

Against the judge's brief, in its own order:

1. **It is a weird job title.** Seven words naming one odd job, nothing else.
2. **It is stranger than ordinary work.** A senior vice president — the most
   ordinary corporate title there is — whose whole domain is waiting-room
   magazines, the most neglected print on earth.
3. **The twist lands on the first read.** Everyone has sat in front of that
   stack: tattered, years out of date, read by no one. Giving it an
   executive is instantly funny.
4. **It sounds like a real job.** You can picture the person: a suit,
   a clipboard, solemnly curating the 2013 National Geographics.
5. **It reads instantly.** One glance, one picture, no explanation needed.

## Run it

```sh
mkdir -p out && MAKEFUN_OUTPUT_DIR=out node main.ts
# writes out/entry.txt, exactly: Senior Vice President of Waiting Room Magazines
cat out/entry.txt  # verify: one line, no trailing newline
```

## How it runs

1. `main.ts` (Node 22, standard library only) holds the title as one
   constant and writes it, byte for byte, to `$MAKEFUN_OUTPUT_DIR/entry.txt`
   (default `/output/entry.txt`).
2. No network, no randomness, no clock: both runs write identical bytes.
